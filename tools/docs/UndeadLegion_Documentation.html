1. Overview

Undead Legion is a skeleton army built on one shared humanoid rig. Six class presets, every armour piece and every animation use the same skeleton, so any armour piece can be worn by any skeleton and every animation plays on every character.

  • 6 skeleton classes: Knight, Warrior, Archer, Assassin, Mage, Necromancer, each a ready prefab.
  • 48 armour modules as separate prefabs: helms, chests, gloves, greaves, boots, skirts, robes and pants.
  • 12 weapon prefabs with adjustable grip points, including a skinned recurve bow whose string follows the draw hand.
  • 46 animation clips plus grip pose data: idles, two locomotion sets with root motion, one- and two-handed attacks, a held block, a bow shot, spell casts, class specials, a hit and a stagger reaction, four deaths, additive twitches and empty-hand finger idles.
  • A Humanoid avatar on every character, so the clips also retarget to other humanoids and other humanoid clips play on the skeletons.
  • A demo scene to browse every character, module, weapon and clip.

2. Requirements and setup

  • Unity 6 (6000.4 or newer) with the Universal Render Pipeline (17.4). The materials are URP/Lit.
  • uGUI (included with Unity) for the demo interface. The demo reads input through either the Input System package or the legacy Input Manager.

Import the package, then open UndeadLegion/Demo/Scenes/UndeadLegion_Demo.unity and press Play. To use a character in your own scene, drag a prefab from UndeadLegion/Prefabs/Characters into it. Each prefab already carries its Animator with the shared controller, its default armour, and the weapon and armour components described below.

If the materials appear pink, the project is not using URP: install the Universal RP package and assign a URP asset in Project Settings > Graphics and Quality.

4. Characters

Every character shares the same 68-bone skeleton (Mixamo-style bone names: Hips, Spine, Spine1, Spine2, Neck, Head, Jaw, LeftArm, LeftForeArm, LeftHand, …, full fingers, and two weapon socket bones LeftWeaponSocket / RightWeaponSocket under the hands). Characters stand 1.7 m to the top of the skull, with the hips at 0.98 m.

Wearing and removing pieces

Each character prefab has a SkeletonModules component listing its default set. At runtime:

var modules = character.GetComponent<SkeletonModules>();
modules.Wear(knightHelmPrefab);     // attach any module prefab (bound by bone name)
modules.Remove(knightHelmPrefab);   // a default piece is switched off, a borrowed one destroyed
modules.Set(0, false);              // toggle a default piece by index
modules.SetAll(true);               // every default piece on

Without SkeletonModules, ArmorModule.Wear(modulePrefab, characterRoot) instantiates a piece and binds it to any character with the shared skeleton. When you swap a piece, take off the character's own piece of the same slot first (for example its Helm) so two pieces do not overlap.

A module prefab dropped into a scene on its own is a prop in its rest pose. It animates once it is attached to a character.

Loadouts

SkeletonWeapon on each character holds a list of loadouts (a display name and the items it puts in which hand): Sword + shield, Sword, Axe + round shield, Mace, Dagger, Two daggers, Longsword (2H), Battle axe (2H), Recurve bow, Staff, Wand.

var weapon = character.GetComponent<SkeletonWeapon>();
weapon.Equip(0);        // equip loadout 0 (see weapon.Count / weapon.NameAt(i))
weapon.Clear();         // empty hands

Hand slots and grips

Two transforms meet when a weapon is equipped: the character's hand slot (RightHandSlot / LeftHandSlot, children of the weapon socket bones) and the weapon prefab's child Grip. The weapon is placed so its Grip matches the slot in position and rotation. Move or rotate either one with the gizmo to adjust how a weapon sits in the hand; all six characters share the skeleton, so one slot setup fits all. The Grip convention: +Y along the handle toward the blade, +Z out of the back of the hand.

When a hand holds an item its fingers close into a grip pose; an empty hand plays a relaxed finger idle (the LeftFingers / RightFingers layers, section 8).

The bow

Shoot_01 carries two animation events, BowAttach and BowRelease. Between them the bow's string follows the draw hand and the limbs bend (BowString on the bow prefab); on the release the string snaps back and SkeletonWeapon can launch the held arrow as a simple ArrowProjectile (the demo's shot; no collision or damage). Receivers are optional: the events use DontRequireReceiver.

The one-handed attacks and casts carry the whole body, so they read complete when a character stands; on the move they play on the arm and upper-body layers over the legs of a walk or run (section 8).

8. The Animator controller

AC_Skeleton is shared by every character. Its layers:

LayerBlendingUse
BaseoverrideEvery clip as a full-body state. Idle_01 is the default.
UpperBodyoverride, mask AM_UpperBodyUpper-body clips (wand casts, the shot, Rally) over a locomotion loop.
LeftArm / RightArmoverride, masks AM_LeftArm / AM_RightArmThe per-arm attacks over a locomotion loop; Block_L_Idle held on LeftArm over anything.
TwitchadditiveOne twitch on a trigger (Twitch, TwitchIndex), used by SkeletonTwitch.
TwitchLoop_01..03additive, weight 0A looping twitch each; raise the layer weight to keep one running.
LeftFingers / RightFingersoverride, finger masksThe relaxed finger idles; SkeletonWeapon sets their weight (1 for an empty hand, 0 while holding).
Hitadditive, mask AM_UpperBodyHit reactions added over whatever Base plays, the legs untouched: animator.CrossFadeInFixedTime("Hit_01", 0.05f, animator.GetLayerIndex("Hit"), 0f). Lower the layer weight for a lighter hit.

Every layer other than Base has an Empty default state. To play an arm attack while walking, cross-fade the attack on its arm layer and let Base keep the walk:

animator.CrossFadeInFixedTime("Attack_R_Slice", 0.15f, animator.GetLayerIndex("RightArm"));

Two-handed attacks, staff casts, specials and deaths are full-body and belong on Base. SkeletonGripState on the two-handed and staff states closes the second hand on the weapon.

11. The demo scene

UndeadLegion_Demo lists the characters (top left), the armour modules of the current character and the sets to borrow from (left) and the animations by section (right). Click a clip to play it; the arm attacks go on their arm layer when a locomotion loop is running, and the block button holds the block until clicked again. The twitch and finger-idle entries are toggles. The footer switches root motion and the turntable and recenters the character. Drag to orbit the camera, scroll to zoom.

12. Using your own animations

The characters use Unity's Humanoid avatar, so any Humanoid clip plays on them: set the clip's rig to Humanoid and add it to the controller. The pack's clips also drive the finger, jaw and extra spine bones; third-party clips leave those at rest. To use the pack's clips on another Humanoid character, assign that character's avatar to the Animator and use AC_Skeleton or your own controller.
