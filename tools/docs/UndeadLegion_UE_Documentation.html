1. Overview

Undead Legion is a skeleton army built on one shared skeleton. Six class presets, every armor piece and every animation use the same skeleton asset, so any armor piece can be worn by any skeleton and every animation plays on every character.

  • 6 skeleton classes: Knight, Warrior, Archer, Assassin, Mage, Necromancer, each a ready Blueprint Character.
  • 48 armor modules as separate skeletal meshes: helms, hoods, chests, gloves, greaves, boots, skirts, robes and pants.
  • 12 weapons as Blueprints spawned on the hand sockets, with adjustable grips; the recurve bow is skinned.
  • 58 animations at 30 fps: idles, two locomotion sets with root motion, one- and two-handed attacks with heavy variants, a held block, a bow shot, casts, specials, hit reactions, staggers, deaths, additive twitches and finger idles. A montage for every one-shot.
  • One Animation Blueprint with a layer stack: base loop, upper body, left arm, right arm, additive twitches, finger layers.
  • Rigged to the Epic skeleton: the UE5 mannequin's bone hierarchy, names and bone frames, so any mannequin animation plays on the characters as it plays on the mannequin (section 12).
  • A demo map with an animation browser and an overview map.

2. Requirements and setup

Unreal Engine 5.8. The package is a complete Blueprint project with no C++ and no plugin dependencies. Open UndeadLegion.uproject and press Play: the startup map is the animation browser.

Adding the pack to your own project: with both editors closed copy the Content/UndeadLegion folder into your project's Content folder (or use Asset Actions > Migrate on the UndeadLegion folder). Everything the pack references lives inside that one folder. The demo's Game Mode is Demo/Blueprints/BP_DemoGameMode; set it on the demo map's World Settings if your project overrides the default.

Rendering: the materials are standard lit materials (one master, instances per character and weapon). The project ships with virtual shadow maps and Lumen off and static lighting disabled, which is the setting the demo was tuned for; the assets work under any renderer setting. The eyes are an unlit emissive material and glow through a Bloom post-process volume in the maps.

4. Characters

Every character shares the skeleton SKEL_UndeadLegion: the UE5 mannequin's hierarchy (root, pelvis, spine_01–spine_05, neck_01 / neck_02, head, clavicle / upperarm / lowerarm / hand with their twist bones, the metacarpals and fingers, thigh / calf / foot / ball with their twist bones, the ik_hand_* and ik_foot_* bones) with the mannequin's bone frames and our proportions, plus four extra bones: jaw_01 / jaw_02 under the head and pelvis_l / pelvis_r under the pelvis — 93 bones in all. Characters stand 170 cm to the top of the skull with the pelvis at 98 cm, feet on Z 0, facing +Y in the mesh (the Blueprint turns the mesh so the character faces its +X, exactly like the mannequin).

Each class is a child of BP_UndeadSkeleton (a Character: capsule, Character Movement, the Animation Blueprint, the eyes) and sets only its own data: DefaultModules (the armor set above), StartLoadout and DisplayName. Drop one into a level and it plays its idle; a spawned character is auto-possessed by an AI controller so that Character Movement runs (root motion needs it).

Each character has two material instances, MI_<Class>_Body and MI_<Class>_Armor, on the master material, with 1024×1024 base color, normal and packed metallic / roughness maps. Armor instances are two-sided, so torn cloth and open hoods read correctly from behind.

Eyes. Two small emissive spheres sit in the eye sockets (sockets EyeL / EyeR on the head bone). EyeColor (HDR, default a glowing red) and EyesVisible are variables on the character; TintEyes(Color) and ShowEyes(On) change them at runtime, for example for an army's team color.

5. Armor modules

Every armor piece is its own skeletal mesh on the shared skeleton, in Characters/Skeleton<Class>/Armor. A piece worn by a character is a skeletal mesh component that follows the body as its leader pose, so a Warrior can wear the Knight's helm or the Mage's robe, on any animation.

Wearing and removing pieces

The functions on the character take the module's skeletal mesh asset:

WearModule(Module)            attach any module (its material comes with the mesh)
RemoveModule(Module)          take a piece off (a default piece is switched off, a borrowed one removed)
IsModuleWorn(Module) -> Worn
SetDefaultModulesWorn(Worn)   the character's own set, all on or all off

When you swap a piece, take off the character's own piece of the same slot first (for example its Helm) so two pieces do not overlap. A module placed in a level on its own is a prop in its rest pose; it animates once it is worn.

Hand sockets and grips

Two transforms meet when a weapon is equipped: the body's socket RightHandSlot / LeftHandSlot (on hand_r / hand_l, the same values on all six bodies) and the weapon Blueprint's mesh component offset. Move either one to change how a weapon sits in the hand: the socket in the skeletal mesh editor (copy it to the other five bodies, they share the skeleton), the mesh component in the weapon Blueprint. The socket convention: +Y along the handle toward the blade, +Z out of the back of the hand.

When a hand holds an item its fingers close into the grip pose (from A_Grip); an empty hand plays a relaxed finger idle (the finger layers, section 8). Two-handed clips hold the second hand on the shaft themselves: SetClipHold(Left, Right) closes a hand that holds nothing while such a clip plays (the demo does this for the two-handed attacks, the two-handed idle and the staff cast).

The bow

Shoot_01 carries two notifies, BowAttach (frame 15, the draw hand reaches the string) and BowRelease (frame 21). BP_Weapon_H2Recurvebow carries a poseable copy of the skinned bow (BowPose) whose bones Nock and Limb_U1/U2, Limb_L1/L2 can be driven from the draw hand between the two notifies, and Demo/Blueprints/BP_ArrowProjectile is a ready arrow actor (a projectile movement at 15 m/s, no gravity, 4 s life). The demo browser plays the shot as a plain one-shot; the string pull and the fired arrow are left for your own gameplay code, the notifies mark the window.

The one-handed attacks and casts carry the whole body, so they read complete when a character stands; on the move they play on the arm and upper-body slots over the legs of a walk or run (section 8). The twitches and the hits are additive against their own first frame. Animations/Montages/AM_<Clip> holds a ready montage for every one-shot (33), each in the slot the clip belongs to, for your own Animation Blueprints; the pack's own character creates its montages at runtime from the sequences.

8. The Animation Blueprint

ABP_UndeadSkeleton is a flat layer stack rather than a state machine, so every clip can be driven by name from Blueprint and you can build your own state machine on the same sequences. From the bottom up:

LayerWhat plays thereDriven by
Basethe looping clips (idles, locomotion, the held block's body) as looping montages in slot Base, and full-body one-shots in DefaultSlotPlayLoop, PlayAction with slot DefaultSlot
Upper bodylayered per bone from spine_04: slot UpperBody (wand casts, the shot, Rally over a walk)PlayAction, slot UpperBody
Left armlayered from clavicle_l: slot LeftArm, and the held block (Block_L_Idle blended in by BlockAlpha)PlayAction, slot LeftArm; SetHeldBlock
Right armlayered from clavicle_r: slot RightArmPlayAction, slot RightArm
Twitchesthree additive loops, Twitch_01..03, each with its own weightSetTwitch(Index, Weight)
HitsHit_01 / Hit_02 as additive one-shots over everythingPlayOverlay (the demo's Reactions buttons)
Fingersper hand, layered from the metacarpal and thumb bones: the relaxed finger idle on an empty hand, the grip fist on a full oneautomatic on equip; SetFingerIdle(Left, On), SetFingersSuspended

A loop and a one-shot play together (the one-shot's montage does not stop the base loop), so a stab on the right-arm slot plays over a walk with the walk's legs and spine, and a shield block held on the left arm stays up through walks and right-hand attacks. Played from standing, the same attack in DefaultSlot reads whole, with its step. The demo browser routes each clip this way: a masked clip goes to its slot while a locomotion loop runs and plays full body otherwise.

9. Root motion and speeds

The locomotion loops and the two staggers carry root motion on the root bone; everything else keeps the root still, so hits and deaths displace the body in the pose. Character Movement reads root motion from montages only (the character's root-motion mode), which is why the loops are played as montages; a sequence player in the graph would not move the capsule. SetRootMotion(false) switches the character to ignore root motion and the loops play in place; the clips then suit an in-place blend space driven by your movement speed, with the measured speeds below. The travel per loop was measured on the exported clips and is identical on all six characters.

10. The character Blueprint API

All functions live on BP_UndeadSkeleton and are callable on every class:

FunctionDoes
PlayLoop(Clip)plays a looping sequence on the base layer (replaces the current loop with a blend)
PlayAction(Clip, Slot, BlendIn, BlendOut, Rate) → Lengthplays a one-shot in DefaultSlot, UpperBody, LeftArm or RightArm at a play rate; returns the clip's length divided by the rate
PlayOverlay(Clip)plays an additive hit over whatever is playing
StopActions(BlendOut), PauseActionsend every one-shot (the loop keeps playing); freeze the pose, for example a corpse
SetHeldBlock(Held)raise or lower the shield block on the left arm, held over anything
SetTwitch(Index, Weight)weight of twitch loop 1..3 (0 off, 1 full)
SetRootMotion(On)root motion drives the capsule, or every clip plays in place
EquipLoadout(Index), GetLoadoutthe loadouts of section 6
WearModule, RemoveModule, IsModuleWorn, SetDefaultModulesWornthe armor of section 5
SetClipHold(Left, Right)close a hand that holds nothing while a two-handed clip plays
SetFingerIdle(Left, On), GetFingerIdle, SetFingersSuspended(Suspended)the relaxed finger idle per hand; suspend both (the demo does so for a death)
ShowEyes(On), TintEyes(Color)the glowing eyes

Variables you may set on an instance or a child class: DefaultModules, StartLoadout, DisplayName, EyeColor, EyesVisible, StartActionDelay / StartActionGap (the idle a placed character starts with and how it spaces its first actions).

11. The demo map

Demo/Maps/UndeadLegion_Demo is the startup map, an animation browser: pick a class at the top left; the armor panel lists the character's own set as toggles with All / None and every other class's set to borrow from; the weapons panel the eleven loadouts; the animation list is grouped as in section 7. A masked clip (right arm, left arm, upper body) clicked while a locomotion loop plays goes to its slot over the walk; otherwise it plays full body and the list's idle returns after it. The block, the twitches and the hand idles are toggles. Deaths hold the corpse for two seconds with the twitches and finger idles off. The footer has Root motion, Turntable, Recenter and Eyes. Drag with either mouse button to orbit, wheel to zoom. Demo/Maps/UndeadLegion_Overview shows all six characters side by side.

The browser is Demo/Blueprints/BP_DemoShowcase with the widgets in Demo/UI; BP_OrbitPawn is the camera and BP_DemoGameMode ties them together. None of it is needed by the characters.

12. Using your own animations

Mannequin animations. The skeleton is the UE5 mannequin's, so an animation made for the mannequin (Manny / Quinn, the Epic marketplace and Fab packs, your own mocap retargeted to the mannequin) plays on the characters as it plays on the mannequin. Three ways to use them, pick one:

  • Compatible skeletons (no copies): open your mannequin's skeleton asset (SK_Mannequin) and add SKEL_UndeadLegion to its Compatible Skeletons (or the other way round). Every mannequin sequence and montage then plays on the characters directly — PlayLoop / PlayAction accept them, and so does any state machine you build.
  • Assign Skeleton: in a character's skeletal mesh asset choose Assign Skeleton → your SK_Mannequin. The mesh joins your mannequin skeleton (its four extra bones are added to it) and uses your animations with nothing else to do.
  • IK Retargeter: with an identical hierarchy the retargeter's chains map automatically; use it when you want baked copies.

The twist bones carry a share of the limb weights, so a mannequin clip twists the forearms and thighs the way it does on the mannequin; the pack's own clips carry their twist on the limb bones.

Your own clips. Import animation FBX files against Characters/SKEL_UndeadLegion (set it as the skeleton in the import dialog) and they play on every character and every armor module. Clips you add are played by the same functions: PlayLoop for loops, PlayAction with a slot for one-shots. To use the pack's clips in your own Animation Blueprint, the montages in Animations/Montages are already in the right slots.

Weapon-specific clips expect the matching loadout: the two-handed attacks and idle hold the second hand on the shaft, the bow shot needs the bow in the left hand and the arrow in the right, the staff cast both hands on the staff.

13. Technical details

  • Skeleton: one asset, the UE5 mannequin's 89 bones (hierarchy, names and bone frames, ik bones included) plus jaw_01 / jaw_02 and pelvis_l / pelvis_r: 93 bones rooted at root. Up to 8 bone influences per vertex. Rigged to the Epic skeleton.
  • Scale: 1 uu = 1 cm, feet on Z 0; the bodies are 169.5 cm tall to the skull, helmets reach about 181 cm.
  • Meshes: 55 skeletal meshes (6 bodies, 48 modules, the bow) and 11 static meshes, one LOD each, triangulated. Bodies 6,364–7,588 triangles, modules 191–6,582, a dressed character 16,728–18,870, weapons 344–1,495 (the bow 3,869).
  • Materials: M_UndeadLegion_Master with 25 instances (12 character, 12 weapon, 1 demo floor) and M_Eyes (unlit emissive). 67 textures: base color (sRGB), normal (DirectX, Y−) and a packed map with metallic in R and roughness in A; 1024² for the characters, 512² for the weapons, 128² for the arrow, every one a power of two.
  • Collision: the characters collide through their capsule; PA_UndeadLegion is the import's physics asset on every body (a capsule per bone, not a tuned ragdoll). The weapon static meshes carry no collision primitives.
  • Root motion: on the root bone for the loops and staggers; locked root (in place) on everything else. Twitches and hits are additive against their own first frame.
  • Project: Blueprint only, no C++, no plugins. Startup and default map UndeadLegion_Demo; the renderer settings in Config/DefaultEngine.ini are the demo's (no static lighting, Lumen and virtual shadow maps off, DX12 SM6).
  • Mobile: the pack renders under the ES3.1 feature level (Unreal's Mobile Preview): the master material uses default mobile features, every texture is a power of two, and the demo maps carry a sky dome, which the mobile renderer needs to show the SkyAtmosphere. It has not been cooked for or run on a device.
