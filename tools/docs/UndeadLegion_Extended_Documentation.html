1. Overview

The Extended Edition contains the complete Undead Legion pack (characters, armour modules, weapons, animations and the browser demo, described in UndeadLegion/Documentation/UndeadLegion_Documentation.pdf) and adds the systems that turn the skeletons into a playable army. Everything new lives in UndeadLegion/Extended; the base assets are used as they are.

  • Full-body ragdolls: 11 rigidbodies with joints on every character, used as hitboxes while animated, with an impulse on death and a blend back to the animation for revives and get-ups.
  • Hit reactions: physical flinches layered over any clip, so a struck skeleton recoils while it keeps walking or attacking.
  • Cloth: robes, skirts and pants simulate, pinned at the waist and colliding with the legs.
  • Rise and dissolve effects: skeletons rise out of the ground and turn to dust, with a glowing edge. Glowing eyes in any colour.
  • NavMesh movement: an agent drives directional locomotion blend trees built from the pack's measured walk, run, back and strafe clips.
  • Combat AI: finds the nearest enemy, closes in and attacks with the moves that suit the equipped weapon: melee, arrows, spells and an area cast.
  • Army spawner: formations of varied skeletons with mixed armour, suitable loadouts, tints and team colours.
  • An arena demo: two armies, a battle, and a skeleton you can take over; and the animation browser with the extended characters, cloth, hits, ragdolls and the effects.

2. Requirements and setup

  • Unity 6 (6000.4 or newer), Universal Render Pipeline 17.4.
  • The AI Navigation package (com.unity.ai.navigation, 2.x) for the NavMesh. It is listed in the project manifest; if you import the package into another project, install it from the Package Manager.

Open UndeadLegion/Extended/Scenes/UndeadLegion_Extended_Demo.unity and press Play. To use the extended skeletons in your own scene, bake a NavMesh there (add a NavMesh Surface to your level and press Bake), then drag a prefab from UndeadLegion/Extended/Prefabs onto it, or add an ArmySpawner.

The extended prefabs are prefab variants of the base characters: changes to a base prefab (its armour set, its hand slots) carry over to its extended variant.

5. Ragdolls

While the character animates, the ragdoll bodies are kinematic and follow the bones, so their colliders work as hitboxes (a raycast or a trigger tells you which body part was struck). Activate(impulse, point) hands the body to physics: the Animator and the root capsule switch off, every body keeps the velocity it had in the animation, the impulse is applied to the body nearest the point, and held weapons drop with their own physics (dropWeapons). BlendToAnimation() moves the character under the fallen hips and blends the pose back to the Animator over blendTime seconds, for revives and get-ups. The bodies are generated by Build() from the humanoid bones; totalMass (35 kg) is spread by the usual ragdoll proportions.

var ragdoll = skeleton.GetComponent<SkeletonRagdoll>();
ragdoll.Activate(hitDirection * 80f, hitPoint);     // fall with a push
ragdoll.BlendToAnimation();                         // stand back up on the animation

6. Hit reactions

SkeletonHitReaction.Hit(point, impulse) kicks damped angular springs on the struck bone and, with falloff, on its parents up to the hips (spine, chest, neck, head, shoulders and arms react). The springs are applied after the Animator every frame, so a hit reads on top of whatever plays and settles back by itself. stiffness sets how fast a bone returns, damping how much it wobbles, strength how far it moves per unit of impulse, maxAngle the largest offset. SkeletonHealth calls it on every hit, so you rarely need to call it directly.

8. Cloth

SkeletonCloth adds Unity Cloth to the worn armour pieces named in its rules (Skirt, Robe and Pants by default). Each cloth is pinned at the waist and frees gradually towards the hem: pinnedBand metres below the waist stay fixed, the hem may move up to maxDistance from its skinned position. The legs' ragdoll capsules are the colliders, so a robe swings around the legs instead of through them. The Knight's set is plate (rigidSets): its pieces never simulate, also when another skeleton wears them. Call Refresh() after changing the worn armour, and SetSimulate(false) to switch cloth off. Beyond cullDistance from the main camera the cloth stops simulating.

9. Rise and dissolve effects, glowing eyes

SkeletonDissolve.DissolveIn() raises a skeleton out of the ground; DissolveOut(destroyAfter) turns it to dust from the top down. For the duration of an effect each renderer (body, armour, held and dropped weapons) uses a copy of its material on the UndeadLegion/Lit Dissolve shader, a URP Lit shader with a cut height, noise and a glowing band; the original materials come back when a rise ends, and per-renderer colours (tints) are kept. Set duration, edgeColor (HDR), edgeWidth and noiseScale to taste; dissolveInOnEnable plays the rise whenever the object is enabled (useful with object pools).

SkeletonEyes (a base-pack script, on every character prefab) places two small glowing spheres in the eye sockets and follows the head. The colour is HDR: values above 1 glow with Bloom (the arena uses URP's Bloom post effect). SetColor() changes it at runtime; addLight adds a faint light at the face, off by default because every light costs in a crowd.

10. NavMesh movement

SkeletonNavController reads the NavMeshAgent's velocity into the controller's MoveX / MoveZ parameters (metres per second in the character's frame) and plays one of two 2D directional blend trees on the Base layer:

GaitStateClips (walk / run / back / strafe)Walk / run speed
HeavyLocomotion_Heavythe shambling _01 set0.52 / 1.14 m/s
UprightLocomotion_Uprightthe upright _02 set1.28 / 1.87 m/s

The agent plans the path and steers; the clips' own root motion moves and turns the body (the agent follows it and keeps it on the NavMesh), so the feet plant and the body turns with each step as authored. The blend-tree positions are the clips' measured speeds, so the wanted speed picks the matching clip. The agent's speed is set from the gait (walk or run). When the skeleton stops, it plays the idle that suits its weapon (Idle_1H_Combat, Idle_TwoHanded, Idle_Bow, Idle_Staff); while it moves, the same weapon pose plays on the UpperBody layer so a two-hander or a bow is still held properly.

var nav = skeleton.GetComponent<SkeletonNavController>();
nav.MoveTo(destination, running: true);
nav.FaceTowards(enemy.position);
nav.PlayAction("Taunt_01");                 // a one-shot; a step in the clip moves the agent
nav.OnLoadoutChanged();                     // after equipping another weapon

11. Combat AI

SkeletonAI picks the nearest living enemy within sightRange (or your target), runs toward it and slows to a walk walkWithin metres (0.8) outside its attack's reach (archers keep walking), stops at the range of its attacks (melee: meleeRange, 1.1 m centre to centre), turns to face it and attacks, waiting attackCooldown seconds between attacks. Damage lands at the moment in the clip where the blow connects. The attacks come from attack modules (AttackModule, the attackModules list on the component): each module names the weapon loadouts it attaches to, and a skeleton fights with the moves of every module that matches what it holds, picking one at random (never the same twice in a row when it has a choice). So swords and daggers use regular attacks only, axes and maces mix regular and heavy ones, and a second dagger adds the left-hand attacks. A heavy module is skipped when allowHeavy is off, which it is on the assassin: it fights with regular attacks whatever it holds. Add a module, or change one's weapons and moves, in the inspector to give a weapon its own set.

damageScale and attackSpeed tune difficulty; wanderRadius lets idle skeletons roam around their start point; magicColor colours the spells. Disable the component to take control of a skeleton (the arena demo does this when you select one).

12. Army spawner

ArmySpawner places count skeletons in a formation of columns × rows, spacing metres apart, snapped to the NavMesh and facing the spawner's forward. Each one gets:

  • a random character from characters;
  • a mixed armour set from armourCatalogue: each slot keeps the class's own piece or borrows another class's (mixChance), within the dressing rules of ArmourRules: the mage and the necromancer always wear a robe and a hood (the robe replaces chest, skirt and pants); the melee classes never wear a robe or a hood, except the assassin, who may wear the mage's hood; a melee skeleton always keeps a chest piece and leaves any other slot empty more often (meleeBareChance, 0.3) than a caster (bareChance, 0.1);
  • a loadout that suits its class (randomLoadout), a subtle colour variation (tintStrength), the team's eyeColor and team;
  • the upright gait for casters and the heavy gait for the rest (gaitByClass);
  • a rise from the ground with a random delay (riseFromGround, riseStagger), the AI switching on when it has risen.

Spawn() and Clear() are also in the component's context menu; spawned lists the skeletons it created.

13. The arena demo

Two armies face each other on a small arena with pillars and walls. The panel raises 8 or 16 skeletons per side, starts and stops the battle, clears the field, and switches cloth, the death mode and the dissolving of corpses.

14. The extended animation browser

UndeadLegion/Extended/Scenes/UndeadLegion_Extended_Showcase.unity is the base pack's animation browser with the extended characters and a PHYSICS panel: cloth on or off, a physical hit, a ragdoll death, revive, rise from the ground and turn to dust. Click the skeleton to hit it where you click; Shift + click kills it into a ragdoll pushed from that point, and clicks on the fallen body push it around. Picking any clip, or Revive, stands a fallen skeleton back up. The browser shows one character at a time, so the component ExtendedShowcase removes its navigation and AI when it spawns; the Hit_01 and Stagger_01 clips are in the Reactions section, and armour pieces put on from the modules panel get cloth.

15. Performance

  • Ragdoll bodies are kinematic until death, so a living skeleton costs only its colliders.
  • Cloth is the most expensive feature: keep cullDistance modest, or remove SkeletonCloth from background crowds.
  • The rise and dissolve material copies are shared per source material and only used during an effect.
  • Eyes are two unlit spheres; their optional light is off by default.
  • For large crowds, set the agents' obstacle avoidance quality lower and consider disabling SkeletonTwitch and the hit reactions far from the camera.
